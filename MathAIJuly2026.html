Nov 2022–2023: Language Models Acquire Tools

  • November 2022: ChatGPT made instruction-following language models widely usable.
  • March 2023: GPT-4 improved abstract reasoning; ChatGPT plugins added a browser, retrieval and a sandboxed Python interpreter.
  • June–November 2023: function calling and the Assistants API made tool use programmable and repeatable.
  • For mathematics, the model no longer had to do everything in its weights: it could calculate, enumerate cases, retrieve facts and test conjectures.
  • But: tool calls were usually short and human-directed; there was no medal-level IMO result from a general-purpose model.

2024: Specialized Systems Reach IMO Silver

  • January: AlphaGeometry solved 25 of 30 historical Olympiad geometry problems by combining a language model with a symbolic deduction engine.
  • July: AlphaProof and AlphaGeometry 2 solved four of six IMO problems for 28/42, the silver-medal threshold.
  • AlphaProof translated problems into Lean and used reinforcement learning to search for machine-checked proofs.
  • This was a decisive reliability advance: the verifier rejected invalid proof steps exactly.
  • Limitation: a specialized formal pipeline, expensive search and manual formalization—not yet a general mathematical conversationalist.

Early 2025: Reasoning, Tools and Agents Converge

  • January–March: Operator, deep research, the Responses API and Agents SDK made multi-step action loops practical.
  • April: o3 and o4-mini could reason about when and how to use web search, Python, files and visual inputs.
  • A tool call answers: what computation should I run now?
  • An agent loop additionally decides: what subgoal comes next, what evidence is missing, and when should I retry or stop?
  • For mathematics this enables experimental search, literature checking, counterexample hunting and independent verification inside one workflow.

Disproving Erdős's Unit-Distance Conjecture

  • Let \(u(n)\) be the maximum number of unit-distance pairs among \(n\) points in the plane.
  • Erdős's lattice construction gave lower bounds of the form \(n^{1+o(1)}\); he conjectured that \(u(n)=n^{1+o(1)}\).
  • An OpenAI model constructed infinitely many configurations with \[ u(n) \geq n^{1+\delta} \] for a fixed \(\delta>0\), disproving this conjecture.
  • The key move replaces Gaussian integers by number fields with richer symmetries, using infinite class-field towers and Golod–Shafarevich theory.
  • This was a new and unexpected bridge from algebraic number theory to discrete geometry (quoting ChatGPT).

The Cycle Double Cover Conjecture

  • Every finite bridgeless graph was conjectured to possess a multiset of cycles covering each edge exactly twice.
  • A 2026 AI-generated proof first reduces to cubic graphs and chooses a nowhere-zero \(\mathbb F_2^3\)-flow.
  • Each edge receives a two-element subset of \(\mathbb F_2^3\); locally, every label must occur zero or twice at each vertex.
  • The local assignments are made globally consistent by solving a linear system, with solvability proved by duality.
  • The resulting proof is only three pages: a striking new encoding and reduction built from established flow theory and linear algebra.

What These Achievements Establish

  • AI can discover original constructions and pursue initially implausible directions.
  • It can make unexpected connections between distant fields.
  • It can coordinate many exploratory approaches and compress the successful path into a short proof.
  • Thus, the remaining human advantage cannot simply be described as creativity or originality.

Where is AI Still Behind?

  • AI can now solve the hardest competition problems and important open research problems.
  • The remaining gap is not simply originality: recent AI proofs contain genuinely new constructions, reductions, and unexpected connections.
  • Two clearer gaps are:
    • Scale: maintaining a coherent programme across many papers and hundreds of pages.
    • New mathematical technologies: inventing objects and methods that reorganize a field.

AI Novelty: Two Recent Examples

  • Unit-distance conjecture:
    • Replace Gaussian integers by number fields with richer symmetries.
    • Use infinite class-field towers and Golod–Shafarevich theory.
    • A surprising new bridge from deep algebraic number theory to discrete geometry.
  • Cycle double cover:
    • Start from a nowhere-zero \(\mathbb F_2^3\)-flow.
    • Invent a two-element-set labelling and reconcile local labels by linear duality.
    • A striking new reduction using established machinery.

Human Invention: New Geometric Objects

  • Gromov: pseudoholomorphic curves. Replace integrable complex structures by tame almost-complex structures and study \(J\)-holomorphic curves.
  • This created moduli spaces sensitive to symplectic rigidity, leading to non-squeezing, Gromov–Witten invariants, Floer theory, and much of contact topology.
  • Schramm: SLE. Encode a random planar interface by the Loewner equation; conformal invariance and the domain Markov property force a Brownian driving process.
  • A two-dimensional random curve becomes a one-dimensional stochastic process, giving a common proof technology for scaling limits in statistical mechanics.

Other Field-Creating Technologies

  • Donaldson: moduli spaces of anti-self-dual connections as invariants of smooth four-manifolds.
  • Floer: infinite-dimensional Morse theory, with differentials defined by counting PDE trajectories.
  • Thurston: hyperbolic structures, laminations, train tracks, Dehn surgery, and geometrization as a new view of 3-manifolds.
  • Cohen: forcing as a reusable machine for constructing new universes of set theory.
  • Scholze: perfectoid spaces and tilting between characteristic zero and characteristic \(p\).
  • Hairer: regularity structures as a calculus for singular stochastic PDEs.

The Serre Conjecture Proof

  • Khare–Wintenberger move through compatible systems and residual characteristics.
  • They combine weight reduction, killing ramification, good-dihedral primes, and modularity-lifting theorems.
  • The global architecture is a double induction on level complexity and residual characteristic/weight.
  • This is a profound new route through existing technologies, rather than a new kind of object comparable to SLE or pseudoholomorphic curves.
  • It is therefore qualitatively closer to the strongest work AI has already demonstrated.

LeanLang

  • With a flood of AI generated proofs, checking them becomes a significant challenge.
  • This was especially so for problems not significant enough to motivate others to check.
  • Fortunately the development of formal verification tools, specifically Lean and Mathlib, has allowed checking to be reduced to checking statements.
  • In many cases, AI can generate Lean proofs.
  • For example, when I told it to use the online Lean server, ChatGPT (GPT-5.6-Sol) one-shotted a Lean proof of the counterexample to the Jacobian Conjecture in about 15 minutes.

Lean Prover

  • Lean is a functional programming language with a fast runtime which is self-hosting.
  • The type system of Lean is based on the Calculus of Inductive Constructions (CIC), which is a dependent type theory.
  • This allows arbitrary mathematical statements to be expressed as types.
  • Theorems and proofs thus are essentially definitions, with types representing statements and values representing proofs.

Lean Prover

  • Formalized proofs are verified by the Kernel, which is a relatively small component. Lean also has independent kernels.
  • Formalization in Lean gives not only guaranteed correctness but also modularity with enforced abstraction boundaries.
  • Lean has a large and growing library of formalized mathematics, called Mathlib.
  • There are also efforts to have much faster-growing vibe-coded libraries, notably the Tau Ceti project.

Combinatory Autoformalization with Agentic AI

  • AI systems have shown impressive capabilities in Mathematical reasoning.
  • However, they still have (related) limitations:
    • Accuracy: in mathematics errors cascade.
    • Autonomy: as they need human verification.
    • Scalability: limited by needing human guidance.
  • An approach is to use a Formal Proof System for verification, and Autoformalization as a bridge.
  • Our system, LeanAide, follows a compositional approach to autoformalization.

AutoFormalization: Ingredients and Stages

  • Formalize statements and definitions using an LLM given examples with results filtered (local step).
  • For documents/proofs (global steps):
    • Generate/Rewrite in a formalization-friendly style.
    • Generate a structured JSON representation of the mathematical text according to a formal schema.
    • Translate iteratively into Lean code, using:
      • Translation of statements and definitions.
      • Lean meta-programming to build Lean code from Lean theorems and definitions.
      • Automation to complete proofs.

Local Steps: Prompting & Post-processing

  • Suppose we are given the statement of a theorem.
  • We find (say) 20 documentation strings in Lean's mathematical library Mathlib that are closest to the statement (for OpenAI embeddings) and include them in the prompt.
  • We query GPT-4 with the prompt for (say) 10 responses (update: GPT-5.6 for 4 responses).
  • We discard responses that are not valid Lean code (do not parse or do not elaborate).

Global Steps: Document to Json

  • We start with a document containing the theorem statement and proof, or just with a statement and get AI to generate the proof.
  • The proof is written/rewritten in a formalization-friendly style.
  • The document is scrutinized by an LLM/agent (like Codex) based on detailed instructions about common mathematical errors and stress-tests.
  • An agentic framework (itself vibe-coded) then generates JSON iteratively based on detailed schemas.

Combinatory Code Generation

  • Code generation is done recursively putting together code generation functions that take in JSON and output Syntax.
  • A function getCode combines these.

LeanAide: Advantages

  • There are many powerful Autoformalization systems such as Aristotle, Gauss and AxiomProver.
  • Coding agents and LLMs have also become good at generating Lean code.
  • However, the nature of LeanAide has advantages:
    • It is extensible and customizable, including incorporating custom tactics.
    • It can be scaled to larger proofs.
    • The generated Lean code is of good quality.
    • Improvements in proof automation and LLMs lead to automatic improvements.

The Future of Mathematics?

  • The rise of Artificial Intelligence in mathematics can cause trouble for:
    • Training.
    • Evaluation.
    • Publication and refereeing.
    • (Public) funding.
  • The consensus response seems to be the Leiden declaration, which was endorsed by the International Mathematical Union (IMU).
  • There are however notable dissenting opinions, such as that of Timothy Gowers.
  • Knowledge beyond a cataloguing of facts depends on abstraction, i.e., the hiding away of details that are not relevant to what we seek to understand and considering only the essential features.
  • For example, when studying a point-mass in mechanics we ignore the colour.
  • Abstraction is done explicitly in working with modular systems in engineering, for instance in software engineering.
  • In all these cases, we abstract by specifying the properties of our system that we are concerned with and studying the system (or its idealised version) in terms of those properties.
  • In mathematics, we turn things on their head and consider fully abstracted systems.
  • Thus, rather than saying that we study systems in terms of the properties of their idealised, abstracted forms, we define our objects of study as those satisfying some properties (or axioms) and think of these as real objects.
  • Going further, we consider common abstractions across a range of (perhaps already abstract) systems, building towers of abstractions.
  • From this viewpoint, the value of a piece of mathematics is a balance between proximity to the empirical and its depth, generality etc.
  • Thus, mathematics (with proofs) can be applied to those aspects of science/engineering that can be abstracted.
  • However, in many cases alternatives to proofs - numerics, simulation, heuristics - are used.
  • In some cases (such as software verification) this is because the mathematics is tedious and labour-intensive; in others it is too hard.
  • With the help of AI, we can automated the tedious, and, one hopes, make feasible the difficult.
