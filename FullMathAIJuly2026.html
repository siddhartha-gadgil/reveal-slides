Rapid Progress in AI Mathematics

  • IMO 2024: AlphaProof and AlphaGeometry 2 reached silver-medal level.
  • IMO 2025: OpenAI and Google DeepMind systems solved five of six problems, reaching gold-medal level in natural language.
  • IMO 2026: GPT-5.6 solved all six problems without human steering.
  • Progress has moved from benchmark mathematics to autonomous contributions at the research frontier.

From Problem Solving to Mathematical Research

  • AI systems now combine long-horizon reasoning, parallel exploration, computation, literature use, and formal verification.
  • They can search for constructions and counterexamples, not merely prove a supplied statement.
  • They have solved or made progress on many open problems, sometimes finding connections specialists did not anticipate.
  • Two striking 2026 examples are the Erdős unit-distance conjecture and the Cycle Double Cover Conjecture.

Disproving Erdős's Unit-Distance Conjecture

  • Let \(u(n)\) be the maximum number of unit-distance pairs among \(n\) points in the plane.
  • Erdős's lattice construction gave lower bounds of the form \(n^{1+o(1)}\); he conjectured that \(u(n)=n^{1+o(1)}\).
  • An OpenAI model constructed infinitely many configurations with \[ u(n) \geq n^{1+\delta} \] for a fixed \(\delta>0\), disproving this conjecture.
  • The key move replaces Gaussian integers by number fields with richer symmetries, using infinite class-field towers and Golod–Shafarevich theory.
  • This was a new and unexpected bridge from algebraic number theory to discrete geometry.

The Cycle Double Cover Conjecture

  • Every finite bridgeless graph was conjectured to possess a multiset of cycles covering each edge exactly twice.
  • A 2026 AI-generated proof first reduces to cubic graphs and chooses a nowhere-zero \(\mathbb F_2^3\)-flow.
  • Each edge receives a two-element subset of \(\mathbb F_2^3\); locally, every label must occur zero or twice at each vertex.
  • The local assignments are made globally consistent by solving a linear system, with solvability proved by duality.
  • The resulting proof is only three pages: a striking new encoding and reduction built from established flow theory and linear algebra.

What These Achievements Establish

  • AI can discover original constructions and pursue initially implausible directions.
  • It can make unexpected connections between distant fields.
  • It can coordinate many exploratory approaches and compress the successful path into a short proof.
  • Thus, the remaining human advantage cannot simply be described as creativity or originality.

Where is AI Still Behind?

  • AI can now solve the hardest competition problems and important open research problems.
  • The remaining gap is not simply originality: recent AI proofs contain genuinely new constructions, reductions, and unexpected connections.
  • Two clearer gaps are:
    • Scale: maintaining a coherent programme across many papers and hundreds of pages.
    • New mathematical technologies: inventing objects and methods that reorganize a field.

AI Novelty: Two Recent Examples

  • Unit-distance conjecture:
    • Replace Gaussian integers by number fields with richer symmetries.
    • Use infinite class-field towers and Golod–Shafarevich theory.
    • A surprising new bridge from deep algebraic number theory to discrete geometry.
  • Cycle double cover:
    • Start from a nowhere-zero \(\mathbb F_2^3\)-flow.
    • Invent a two-element-set labelling and reconcile local labels by linear duality.
    • A striking new reduction using established machinery.

Human Invention: New Geometric Objects

  • Gromov: pseudoholomorphic curves. Replace integrable complex structures by tame almost-complex structures and study \(J\)-holomorphic curves.
  • This created moduli spaces sensitive to symplectic rigidity, leading to non-squeezing, Gromov–Witten invariants, Floer theory, and much of contact topology.
  • Schramm: SLE. Encode a random planar interface by the Loewner equation; conformal invariance and the domain Markov property force a Brownian driving process.
  • A two-dimensional random curve becomes a one-dimensional stochastic process, giving a common proof technology for scaling limits in statistical mechanics.

Other Field-Creating Technologies

  • Donaldson: moduli spaces of anti-self-dual connections as invariants of smooth four-manifolds.
  • Floer: infinite-dimensional Morse theory, with differentials defined by counting PDE trajectories.
  • Thurston: hyperbolic structures, laminations, train tracks, Dehn surgery, and geometrization as a new view of 3-manifolds.
  • Cohen: forcing as a reusable machine for constructing new universes of set theory.
  • Scholze: perfectoid spaces and tilting between characteristic zero and characteristic \(p\).
  • Hairer: regularity structures as a calculus for singular stochastic PDEs.

The Serre Conjecture Proof

  • Khare–Wintenberger move through compatible systems and residual characteristics.
  • They combine weight reduction, killing ramification, good-dihedral primes, and modularity-lifting theorems.
  • The global architecture is a double induction on level complexity and residual characteristic/weight.
  • This is a profound new route through existing technologies, rather than a new kind of object comparable to SLE or pseudoholomorphic curves.
  • It is therefore qualitatively closer to the strongest work AI has already demonstrated.

Does AI Ability Vary Across Fields?

The important divide may not be algebra versus geometry, but:

formal microworlds with exact feedback
versus
open-ended structures with weak feedback.

  • Favourable: explicit representations, discrete operations, cheap examples, and automatic verification.
  • Difficult: long fragile dependencies, multiscale phenomena, singular limits, and the need to invent a representation.

Why Algebra is Amenable

  • Modern algebra provides highly compressed languages: universal properties, functors, exact sequences, adjunctions, and cohomological obstructions.
  • Arguments often become controlled transformations in this language.
  • Their essential content usually survives serialization into text or formal syntax.
  • Finite groups, rings, matrices, and modules provide cheap experiments and counterexamples.
  • But: deep definitions, variance, canonical isomorphisms, and higher coherence create very long-range failure modes.

Geometry is Not Uniformly Disadvantaged

  • Euclidean geometry: incidence, cyclicity, ratios, and angles admit a compact formal vocabulary and exact deduction.
  • AlphaGeometry shows that learned auxiliary constructions plus a symbolic engine can reach Olympiad level without human-like visual imagery.
  • Topology: isotopy, cutting, gluing, surgery, and handle moves are diagrammatic, but often have discrete encodings.
  • Differential and symplectic geometry: intuition concerns curvature, perturbation, compactness, transversality, degeneration, and bubbling—not merely pictures.
  • The deepest ability is often switching between a picture, equations, a combinatorial model, and an invariant.

Will World Models Bridge the Gap?

  • Physical/video world models should help with diagrams, rigid motion, spatial reasoning, flows, and deformation.
  • But physical 3-dimensional intuition can be misleading for high-dimensional, quotient, derived, or infinite-dimensional geometry.
  • The more relevant goal is a mathematical world model: an invariant-aware simulator of mathematical objects and interventions.

The required output is not just the next image, but the next structured mathematical state.

A Mathematical World Model

It should support operations such as:

perturb • deform • quotient • blow up • pass to a cover
rescale • take a limit • perform surgery • compute invariants

  • Multiple synchronized representations: diagrams, equations, combinatorial models, invariants, and formal objects.
  • Quotient-aware identity: equality up to isomorphism, homotopy, gauge, or almost everywhere.
  • Counterfactual experiments: weaken hypotheses, perturb parameters, and search singular limits.
  • Exact verification: attach a prover to approximate intuition and simulation.

Likely Trajectory

  1. Specialized symbolic environments reduce field differences wherever representations and verifiers are mature.
  2. Multimodal mathematical world models improve construction, deformation, degeneration, and counterexample intuition.
  3. The hardest step remains representation invention: deciding that the right objects are pseudoholomorphic curves, SLEs, perfectoid spaces, or regularity structures.
A new mathematical technique often consists precisely in discovering the right state space and the right operations.

Lean and the Remaining Limitations

  • Lean is more than a final proof checker.
  • It provides an interactive mathematical environment with exact state, legal actions, and kernel-checked success.
  • Autoformalization connects the informal mathematical world—where ideas are generated—to this verified environment.
  • Its effect is strongest on accuracy, autonomy, and scale, and more indirect on new concepts and geometric intuition.

Accuracy: What Lean Guarantees

  • Lean prevents invalid deductions, missing hypotheses, quantifier errors, type mismatches, and circular dependencies.
  • Failures are localized at the first invalid interface instead of propagating through a long proof.
  • But Lean proves the formal statement supplied to it.
  • It does not by itself guarantee that the statement, definitions, or axioms faithfully express the intended mathematics.

Kernel correctness must be combined with semantic auditing of autoformalization.

Autoformalization: The Semantic Bottleneck

Informal theorem  \(\longrightarrow\)  Formal statement  \(\longrightarrow\)  Formal proof

  • The second arrow has a kernel-checkable success criterion; the first does not.
  • A mistranslated theorem can have a completely correct Lean proof.
  • Useful safeguards include back-translation, examples and counterexamples, hypothesis mutation, and independent formalizations.
  • Human attention can then focus on principal definitions and statements rather than every inferential step.

Autonomy: Lean as Feedback

propose → elaborate → inspect goals → repair → verify

  • Every proof state exposes the exact goal, local hypotheses, available premises, and remaining subgoals.
  • AI can reject incorrect attempts without asking a human to read them.
  • Formal theorem proving becomes a search or reinforcement-learning environment with an exact reward.
  • This reduces dependence on human proof checking, but not on judgement about which goals and definitions matter.

Scale: Persistent Verified Mathematics

  • Exact memory: a proved theorem retains its statement and dependencies.
  • Abstraction boundaries: later work depends on verified interfaces, not hidden proof details.
  • Parallel decomposition: agents can prove separate lemmas that compose only when their types match.
  • Refactoring: changing a definition reveals precisely which downstream results break.
  • Retrieval: theorem types and dependency graphs supplement linguistic similarity.

The cost is formalization overhead and the need to build missing domain libraries.

Progressive Formalization

  • Formalizing everything at the beginning can freeze a premature representation.
  • A practical research workflow formalizes progressively:
  1. principal definitions and theorem statements;
  2. interfaces between proof components;
  3. delicate constructions, estimates, and calculations;
  4. the final dependency chain.

Informal exploration remains flexible while critical mathematical knowledge becomes persistent and verified.

LeanAide as the Bridge

Mathematical document  →  structured JSON  →  combinatory code generation  →  Lean

  • The structured representation preserves induction, cases, constructions, key lemmas, and estimates.
  • Global proof architecture is separated from local statement translation and proof automation.
  • Code generators enforce reusable proof patterns and can be extended with domain-specific forms and tactics.
  • Errors can be diagnosed at the document, schema, elaboration, tactic, or kernel level.

Can Lean Help Discover New Techniques?

  • Compare candidate definitions by the theorems and closure properties they support.
  • Extract minimal hypotheses and identify recurring proof patterns worth abstracting.
  • Generate variants, examples, counterexamples, and formally verified experiments.
  • Test whether a proposed invariant is well defined and stable under the intended operations.
  • However, Lean does not decide that pseudoholomorphic curves, SLE, or perfectoid spaces are the right objects to introduce.

Lean accelerates refinement and exploitation of a new idea more directly than its initial conception.

Lean and Mathematical World Models

  • Lean is a logical world model: proof states are states, tactics are actions, and the kernel checks success.
  • It does not simulate geometric deformation, PDE evolution, random interfaces, or physical systems.
  • World models and computation can propose pictures, interventions, and conjectures.
  • Autoformalization translates their consequences into precise statements; Lean verifies them.

Exploration supplies candidate meaning; formalization supplies persistent structure; Lean supplies trust.

Lean Prover

  • Lean is a functional programming language with a fast runtime which is self-hosting.
  • The type system of Lean is based on the Calculus of Inductive Constructions (CIC), which is a dependent type theory.
  • This allows arbitrary mathematical statements to be expressed as types.
  • Theorems and proofs thus are essentially definitions, with types representing statements and values representing proofs.

Lean Prover

  • Formalization in Lean thus allows for programs, algorithms, and proofs to be written in a unified framework.
  • We get not only guaranteed correctness but also modularity with enforced abstraction boundaries.
  • Lean is mostly written in Lean and has a pleasant meta-programming interface.
  • Thus, Lean provides a common language that is mathematically and Turing complete, is easily extensible, and with excellent tooling and engineering (e.g., FBIP, IDE support).

Combinatory Autoformalization with Agentic AI

AutoFormalization: Ingredients and Stages

  • Formalize statements and definitions using an LLM given examples with results filtered (local step).
  • For documents/proofs (global steps):
    • Generate/Rewrite in a formalization-friendly style.
    • Generate a structured JSON representation of the mathematical text according to a formal schema.
    • Translate iteratively into Lean code, using:
      • Translation of statements and definitions.
      • Lean meta-programming to build Lean code from Lean theorems and definitions.
      • Automation to complete proofs.

Local Steps: Prompting & Post-processing

  • Suppose we are given the statement of a theorem.
  • We find (say) 20 documentation strings in Lean's mathematical library Mathlib that are closest to the statement (for OpenAI embeddings) and include them in the prompt.
  • We query GPT-4 with the prompt for (say) 10 responses.
  • We discard responses that are not valid Lean code (do not parse or do not elaborate).

Global Steps: Document to Json

  • We start with a document containing the theorem statement and proof, or just with a statement and get AI to generate the proof.
  • The proof is written/rewritten in a formalization-friendly style.
  • The document is scrutinized by an LLM/agent (like Codex) based on detailed instructions about common mathematical errors and stress-tests.
  • An agentic framework (itself vibe-coded) then generates JSON iteratively based on detailed schemas.

Combinatory Code Generation

  • Code generation is done recursively putting together code generation functions that take in JSON and output Syntax.
  • A function getCode combines these.

LeanAide: Advantages

  • There are many powerful Autoformalization systems such as Aristotle, Gauss and AxiomProver.
  • Coding agents and LLMs have also become good at generating Lean code.
  • However, the nature of LeanAide has advantages:
    • It is extensible and customizable, including incorporating custom tactics.
    • It can be scaled to larger proofs.
    • The generated Lean code is of good quality.
    • Improvements in proof automation and LLMs lead to automatic improvements.
